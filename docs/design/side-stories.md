# Side Stories — the found layer

Companion to [`world-bible.md`](world-bible.md). The bible carries **Mir's story,
which the game tells outright**. This document carries **everything else, which
the game never says at all**.

These are complete little tragedies that happened in this world before Mir walked
through it. Each one is staged physically — wreckage, arrangement, absence — and
resolved only in the player's head. None of them are on the critical path. None
of them give stats. Finding one and understanding it *is* the reward.

---

## The rules

**1. The story is in the arrangement, not the objects.** A skeleton is not a
story. A skeleton with its arms around a smaller skeleton, in a room barred from
the inside, is. Every vignette below must be legible from *how things are placed*
— what faces what, what is missing, what was done in a hurry, what was done with
enormous care.

**2. Text is a failure mode, used sparingly and never as narration.** Three
delivery tiers, and prefer the top one:

- **Silent** — pure staging, no text anywhere. The purest and the best. Aim for
  a third of the catalog.
- **Labelled** — one found object with one line *in a character's own voice*,
  which is evidence rather than explanation: a ledger entry, a scratched name, a
  note someone actually wrote. Never a narrator.
- **Echoed** — an echo rune with one line. Reserved for the ones where the
  physical evidence genuinely cannot carry it. If you're writing an echo that
  *describes what the player is looking at*, delete it; the staging failed and
  the fix is in the staging.

**3. Never explain the world.** Old echoes did a lot of cosmology
("the floor that hums," "the sound we were all born owing"). All of that is
retired with the cosmology. These stories are about *people* — what they did when
the water came, what they did about the rules, what they could not put down.

**4. One idea each.** If it needs two beats to understand, it's a scripted scene,
not a side story.

**5. They must be findable by curiosity and missable by hurry.** Off the road,
behind something, down a spur. A player who runs the critical path sees maybe a
fifth of these and should feel the absence at the edges.

**6. Nothing here contradicts the spine, and nothing here is required to
understand it.** These deepen; they never gate.

## The Climber — the one thread that spans the world

**The single most valuable thing in this document.** Five marks, laid along the
whole route, left by the last person to refuse a rule and leave the Fold —
generations before Mir, walking this exact path, carrying a child.

He is **the Harrow**, the man Mir meets at the den at the end of the Scar
(world-bible §5). Nothing ever says so. The marks are just marks. But a player
who has been following them walks into that den already knowing, and that scene
becomes the best in the game for them and a merely good one for everyone else.

The through-line is **the notches** — a mark cut at each stage to measure a
child's height against. Same tool, same hand, five times, climbing.

| # | Region | The mark | What it says without saying it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Fold** | A doorframe at the back of an abandoned house: two notches, an adult's height and a small one. Fresh cuts in old wood, no dust in the grooves. | Someone measured a child here, in a town where children do not grow. Which means they expected the number to change. |
| 2 | **The Kelp Shelf** | The same two notches on a mast, at the same heights. A third cut started and abandoned halfway. | Still measuring. Nothing has moved. Trying anyway. |
| 3 | **The Breach** | On a ticket-booth post: the small notch, cut **higher**. Beside it, scratched hard enough to split the wood, a single vertical stroke. | Air. The boy grew — the first time anything in this world ever did — and the mark for it is a slash, because whoever cut it had no word for it. |
| 4 | **The Scar, early** | On a survey stake at the edge of the first dig: four notches now, climbing, each a good hand apart. The stake is driven into ground that has been turned over and raked flat. | He kept measuring for a while up here. And he was already digging. |
| 5 | **The Scar, the den** | On the doorpost, the same two original heights — adult, and small — cut fresh, over and over, one on top of the other, for so long the post is worn hollow and has been replaced at least twice. | He is still measuring a child who is not there. He has been doing it for generations. He starts every day by cutting the same two marks. |

**Design notes.** All five are silent — no text, ever, including the last. Same
prop kit, same cut angle, so they read as one hand. Each is off the critical path
and takes real curiosity. Nothing tracks them, nothing counts them, no achievement
fires. If a player finds only #5 it is a strange detail at a doorway; if they
found #1 first it is a knife.

## The Fold

The town, before and around the rule. Warm, and wrong. The Fold's stories are
about **obedience** — what it costs, and how people make it bearable.

**F1 — The Baker's Daughter** *(silent)*
The village drowned during its harvest festival; the ring of boats in the plaza
was a blessing rite for the fleet. The water came fast. She got to the moorings
first and cut every rope but her own, and hers ran out of time.
*Staging:* the boat ring, every mooring cut clean — one still tied, straining
outward, the rope frayed where someone sawed at it from the wrong side, in a
hurry, from inside the boat. The Fold's entire ethic in one image: self-sacrifice
curdled into obedience, and honoured as a virtue ever since.

**F2 — Two Bowls** *(silent)*
A house at the town's edge, kept immaculate. Two bowls at the table. One has been
used this morning. The other has not been used in a very long time, and has been
washed anyway, every day, for years.
*Staging:* the second bowl's place is worn pale where a cloth has gone over it.
Nobody in this town can tell you when the second person left, because nobody here
can tell you when anything happened.

**F3 — The Ledger-Keeper's Hands** *(labelled)*
He writes the children's names in the obelisk's book. He has done it for longer
than he can account for.
*Staging:* the ledger, open, chained to its lectern. The last twenty entries are
in a hand that gets worse down the page — not shakier, *slower*, each letter
pressed harder. One line is scratched out entirely and rewritten identically.
*Text (the ledger's own last line):* "I have checked it against yesterday's four
times. It is the same list. I am checking it again."

**F4 — The Rule About Lamps** *(silent)*
Somewhere back in the unrecorded years, a rule was handed down about which way
lamps face. Everyone obeys it. Nobody knows it is a rule; they think it is how
lamps go.
*Staging:* every lamp in the Fold — hundreds, across the whole region — hangs
with its shutter turned the same direction, including the ones where that faces a
wall, including the ones inside houses, including a broken one on the ground
someone has carefully set the right way round.

**F5 — The Counter** *(labelled)*
Someone tried to list every rule the Fold lives under, to see how many there were.
*Staging:* a shed, floor to ceiling in tally marks, in different inks, across
what must be years. Deep into it the marks stop being organised. The final wall
is one continuous scratched field.
*Text (scratched by the door, at head height):* "It is longer than anyone alive.
Do not tell them. They are happy."

**F6 — The Neighbour Who Saw Her Go** *(NPC, told — see world-bible §10)*
Not a silent vignette; a person who mentions, unprompted and early, that Lunal
went out the night the rule was read and came back with mud on her boots from
somewhere there is no mud. Listed here because it is the same *kind* of object —
evidence, offered without interpretation, that means nothing on the way up and
everything on the way down.

**F7 — The Bread That Keeps** *(silent)*
*Staging:* a bakery, fully stocked, every loaf perfect. A stool by the window
where someone has sat for a very long time — the seat worn through to the frame —
facing the door, waiting for a rush that stopped coming when everyone realised
they would never need to buy bread twice.

## The Kelp Shelf

The climb. Its stories are about **weight** — what people carried, cut loose, or
could not put down.

**K1 — Three Steps from the Lift** *(silent)*
The mining town dug into a gallery and stayed too long. The foreman covered his
ears and ran.
*Staging:* a field of salt-wracked figures, all facing the same way, into the
rock. One faces away, mid-stride, three steps from the lift cage, hands still up
at the sides of its head.

**K2 — The Rope, Cut from Above** *(silent)*
Two climbers roped together on a bad traverse.
*Staging:* a mast run with a snapped line. The two ends are findable — the lower
one, far below with a body, and the upper one still knotted to the anchor. The
cut is clean, and it is on the anchor side. Nothing indicates who made the
decision or whether it saved anyone.

**K3 — The Shoe Above the Door** *(silent)*
*Staging:* over the entrance to the mine office, a child's shoe, nailed up. In
the Fold this is done for luck. There is no child anywhere in this region, and
there never was — the mine did not allow them.
*(Optional depth: a second, matching shoe is findable much deeper in, in the
gallery, where it should not be able to have got to.)*

**K4 — The Foreman's Ledger** *(labelled)*
*Staging:* a shift book on a desk, meticulous, columns of names and hours.
*Text (final entry):* "Every shift but mine ends at the sound. I have written
mine down anyway. Someone should know how long I lasted."

**K5 — The Gallery** *(silent)*
The thing they dug into and would not stop listening to. The game never shows
what it is and never will.
*Staging:* a sealed adit, boarded from the *outside* by many hands, over a very
long time — layer on layer of boards, each generation's work over the last, the
newest planks recent. Whatever is behind it, the town's answer was to keep adding
wood forever rather than ever look.

**K6 — The Ones Who Climbed Light** *(echoed)*
*Staging:* an abandoned high camp, gear sorted into two heaps: what was taken,
and what was left. The left heap contains, among the tools and rope, everything
personal — every letter, every keepsake, a wedding band.
*Echo:* "The ones who get highest are the ones who love least. I am not going to
climb well."
*(This is the one echo in this region in Mir's voice. It is placed where he can
see the two heaps.)*

## The Breach

The waterline, staged as a carnival that kept performing after the water came.
Its stories are about **the show going on** — the specific horror of ritual
outliving its purpose.

**B1 — The Ticket-Taker** *(silent)*
*Staging:* the turnstile, and beside it a drift of torn stubs, waist-deep and
still growing outward in a fan, far more than the seats in this place could
account for. Whoever stood here kept taking tickets from the water.

**B2 — Front Row, Both of Us** *(labelled — and it is theirs)*
*Staging:* two ticket stubs, kept together, tucked into a beam in a private
alcove above the ring. Front row, consecutive seats.
*Text (written on the back of one, in a hand the player will see again in the
Keep):* "don't blink."
This is Mir and Lunal's, from before — the Breach was a place people came to,
once. It is the only artefact of the two of them being happy in the entire game,
and it sits forty feet from where she takes his son. Nothing marks it. It is a
side story that turns out not to be one.

**B3 — The Champion's Last Bout** *(silent)*
*Staging:* the ring. The floor is broken upward from beneath. The ropes are
snapped *outward* on the far side. Every chair in the house is tipped and facing
away from the ring, in one direction, as if the audience turned as one and did
not watch the end.

**B4 — The Diver** *(labelled)*
Someone went down after the water came to see how far it went.
*Staging:* a diving rig at the waterline, hoses coiled, everything stowed
correctly, the helmet set down neatly on the boards beside it.
*Text (chalked on the boards beside the helmet):* "It goes all the way. Don't ask
me again."

**B5 — The Carousel** *(silent)*
*Staging:* the carousel is running. Slowly, on the current, in the dark, with no
mechanism attached to anything — the water turns it. Two of the horses are
missing from their poles. The poles are bent outward.

**B6 — The Lost-Child Post** *(silent)*
*Staging:* a painted post — the place a carnival tells families to meet if they
get separated, a hand pointing to a height mark for children. It is intact, and
clean, and someone has recently swept the ground around it. It is nowhere near
the road. Somebody is still maintaining the meeting point.

## The Scar

The surface — and the region with a second meaning. On the way in it reads as
monster country. On the way out, after the den, the player knows the gouges are
tool marks and the whole waste is **one man's search** (world-bible §6.4).

Its vignettes have to work twice: sinister on first read, unbearable on second.
Stage them so nothing has to be re-lit or re-dressed — the *same object* means
something else once you have met him.

**S1 — The Collector's Shelf** *(silent)*
*Staging:* inside the den, a shelf of small objects arranged by size — a shoe, a
comb, a cup, a wooden animal. Dusted. The gaps are sized for things that have
been *taken back off the shelf* and used elsewhere.
This is Lunal's cache. She has been furnishing a nursery.

**S2 — The Camp with Two Bedrolls** *(silent)*
*Staging:* a cold camp, well made, sheltered from the wind. Two bedrolls: one
adult, one very small, the small one nearer the fire. The small one has been
slept in. There is no third bedroll, and no restraint of any kind, anywhere in
this camp.

**S3 — The Boarded Room** *(labelled)*
Someone locked themselves in with the rules.
*Staging:* a room nailed shut from the inside, walls covered in writing —
hundreds of numbered lines, all of them commandments, transcribed from memory.
The numbering has gaps.
*Text (one line, larger, over the door):* "I have lost eleven of them. If I lose
them all, am I free, or am I just breaking them?"

**S4 — The Pilgrims** *(silent)*
*Staging:* a line of prints, single file, adult, all walking *toward* the den's
mouth. None coming out. Bundles set down neatly at the threshold — everything
they were carrying, placed in a row, nothing dropped.

**S5 — The Wrong Small Prints** *(silent — a cruelty)*
*Staging:* a clear, fresh trail of small prints, perfect, exactly the right size.
Followed to its end it resolves into a three-toed den-thing's gait. The player,
by this point, can read it. Mir cannot, yet.

**S6 — The Oasis** *(silent — the one warm thing)*
*Staging:* deep off the road, a pocket of living green: warm moss, a clear
spring, and — the only instance in the entire game — visible growth. New shoots.
Something in bud.
Nothing happens here. There is no reward, no echo, no marker, no fight. It is the
thing Mir is climbing for, in miniature, and it is placed where only a player who
has stopped hurrying will ever stand in it.

**S7 — The Grid** *(silent — reads twice)*
*Staging:* from a rise, a long view over a stretch of the Scar. First read: random
devastation. Held for a second: the gouges run in **parallel lines**, evenly
spaced, turned at the ends like ploughed furrows, and the burned rings sit at
regular intervals along them. Somebody has been working this ground methodically
for a very long time.
The single most important vignette in the region and the one that makes the den
land. Place it where the camera naturally rests, and never point at it.

**S8 — The Tools** *(silent)*
*Staging:* a cache under an overhang — dozens of digging tools, worn down to
stubs, arranged by how much is left of them. The oldest are worn to the socket.
Nobody replaces a tool that many times unless they have been doing one job for
longer than tools last.

**S9 — The Sorted Pits** *(silent — near the den)*
*Staging:* rows of shallow pits, and beside them everything small that has been
dug out of this ground, laid out on cloth. Sorted by size, then by kind. Shoes.
Buttons. Bones too small to be an adult's. All of it clean. All of it kept.
None of it thrown away.
*(This is the Harrow's, not Lunal's — see S1 for hers. His is a landscape; hers
is a room. Do not let the two collections look alike: his is exhaustive and
unsorted-by-meaning, hers is chosen.)*

**S10 — The Gentle Trail** *(silent — the evidence, laid out)*
*Staging:* a long stretch where the two sets of prints run side by side. The
small ones wander. The adult ones stop *every time the small ones stop*, and wait,
and go on. At one point the small set climbs onto a rock and the adult set circles
it. There is no blood anywhere on the Scar, and the player has been noticing that
for an hour.

## The Keep

A drowned hall that Lunal found empty and took. Its dead are **dressing, not
characters** — nobody in this room is anybody's story, which is exactly why she
chose it. The region's vignettes are about a woman living alone somewhere
enormous while she builds one small thing.

**H1 — The Performance** *(silent)*
*Staging:* the hall drowned mid-performance. Stands, chairs, instruments, all
still in position, all still facing the same way. The house seats are full. Not
one of them is turned toward an exit.
Beautiful, and it means nothing. Resist every temptation to give it a story — the
Keep's power is that it is a magnificent room where nothing happened, containing
one small room where everything did.

**H2 — The Swept Aisle** *(silent)*
*Staging:* the hall is filthy, silted, undisturbed — except one path. A single
route, swept clean, from a side door to the stage and back. Walked so many times
the stone is dished. She has been here a long time and she has only ever needed
to go one place.

**H3 — Her Camp** *(silent)*
*Staging:* in a wing off the stage, where she actually lives: a bedroll, a lamp,
a cooking pot, and nothing else — no chair, no table, no comfort of any kind. She
built a warm room with a rug and a cup and a wooden animal in it, forty feet
away, and gave herself a stone floor.

**H4 — The Barred Door** *(silent)*
*Staging:* the hall's main doors, barred — from the inside, with an enormous
amount of work, timber hauled from somewhere else entirely and fitted properly.
Every other entrance to the building is barred the same way. She did not fortify
this place against a monster. She fortified it against **a rule**, and against
one man she knew was coming.

**H5 — The Room** *(silent — and it is the whole game)*
*Staging:* the cage, before the fight, seen from below through the stage boards:
warm light, a rug, a small chair, a cup, a wooden animal from the shelf in S1.
Bars padded with cloth, stitched by hand, carefully, in a pattern. The lock is on
the outside. It is the nicest room in the game. It is the only room anyone in
this world has ever *made* for someone rather than woken up inside.

**H6 — The Measurements** *(labelled)*
*Staging:* pinned beside the cage's frame, a builder's sketch of the room, in
Lunal's hand. Dimensioned. Revised — three times, each revision making it larger.
*Text (margin note):* "He will not need more than this. But make it larger
anyway."
She built it knowing he would never grow, and could not stop herself from
building room for it. She has the same disease as everyone else in this world and
hers looks like love, because it is.

## Counts and coverage

| Region | Silent | Labelled | Echoed | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Fold | 4 | 2 | 0 | 6 *(+1 NPC)* |
| The Kelp Shelf | 4 | 1 | 1 | 6 |
| The Breach | 4 | 2 | 0 | 6 |
| The Scar | 9 | 1 | 0 | 10 |
| The Keep | 5 | 1 | 0 | 6 |
| **The Climber** | 5 | 0 | 0 | 5 |
| **Total** | **31** | **7** | **1** | **39** |

Roughly 80% silent, which is the right ratio. The previous canon ran ~55 echo
lines and zero silent vignettes; that inversion is the point of this document.

The Scar carries the heaviest load (10) because it is the longest region *and*
because its vignettes do double duty — every one of them has to read as monster
country on the way in and as one man's search on the way out.

**Retired:** every old echo about the hum, the unresolved note, the accumulated
grief, the song that owns voices, the chorus resting, and the Conductor's
orchestra, blank scores, bolted chairs, and rehearsal. The first group explained
a cosmology that no longer exists; the second belonged to a character who has
been replaced by a man with a shovel. What survives — the foreman, the ledger,
the shoe, the boat ring, the collector's shelf, the padded bars — is restaged
here as physical evidence rather than narration.

## Open

- **Prop kit cost.** Most of these need bespoke set dressing (a ledger, a
  turnstile drift, a carousel, a measuring notch, the cage interior). Cheapest
  path is to design the prop kit *from this list* rather than fitting stories to
  the props that exist.
- **Placement pass.** Every vignette needs a hand-placed location off the road;
  the current generator scatters echoes procedurally, which is the opposite of
  what a staged vignette needs.
- **How many is too many.** 36 is a good target for the first pass. The risk is
  not quantity, it's dilution — one more forgettable arrangement of barrels costs
  more than it adds.
